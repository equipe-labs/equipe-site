I am Abel, the founder of equipe. I spent ten years writing software for startups, and the question I hear most from friends who do not code is the same one: "Can I build this myself now, with AI?" The honest answer in late 2026 is yes, with one condition. The first screen is easy. Getting it live, keeping it working and changing it next month is where non-coders get stuck. This guide is about choosing a route that handles those parts too.

Everything here comes from things we built or measured: our own cost data from live customer projects, a youth soccer academy that runs its site with an AI team, and an internal example app with real rental listings. Where I talk about other products, the facts come from their own pages, with the date we read them.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> You can build a website or app without coding by using a website builder (Wix, Squarespace), an AI app builder (Lovable, Bolt, Base44), or an AI startup team (equipe) that also puts it live and keeps improving it. Pick by who handles hosting, data, the domain and changes after launch, not by who makes the prettiest first screen.</p>

## Website or app: which one do you need?

The words get mixed up, so here is the practical difference.

- **A website** mostly shows things: who you are, what you sell, how to reach you. A restaurant menu, a portfolio, a landing page with a waitlist.
- **An app** remembers things for people: accounts, bookings, orders, saved items, a dashboard. If a visitor signs in, or you need to see a list of what people did, you are building an app, even if it looks like a website.

Most small businesses need a website with one or two app-like parts: a booking form that saves requests, a list of sign-ups, a member area. That middle ground is exactly where the choice of route matters most.

## What AI can build today, and what still needs you

AI is now good at the first version of most small products: a site with several pages, forms that save to a database, sign-in by email code, an admin page to see the records, a booking request flow, a small shop that hands payment to a card processor.

It still needs a person for:

- **Deciding what matters.** Which customers, which promise, what to leave out.
- **Facts only you know.** Your prices, refund rules, opening hours, the minimum age you coach.
- **Accounts in your name.** A payment processor, a domain, a third-party data service. These are legal and money relationships, so you sign up and approve.
- **Taste and approval.** What goes live, what it costs, what reaches customers.

I wrote more about where the line sits in [can AI build an app for me?](/blog/can-ai-build-an-app-for-me/), with a real example of what an AI team built and where it fell short.

## Step by step: from idea to a live site

1. **Write one sentence.** "A booking page for my dog grooming business where people pick a service and a time." Who it is for and the one thing they do.
2. **List the facts only you know.** Services, prices, hours, policies, your contact email. Five minutes now saves a round of questions later.
3. **Pick the route.** Pages only: a website builder. You want to steer every step: an AI app builder. You want it built, put live and improved for you: an AI team.
4. **Build the smallest version.** One page, one action. Resist adding accounts, payments and a blog on day one.
5. **Put it live properly.** A real address, HTTPS, a privacy policy, a contact form that reaches you, and analytics. On equipe the team does these from [day one](/day-one/); on other routes, use our [launch guide](/blog/launch-a-real-product-without-code/) as a checklist.
6. **Get your own domain.** Our guide to [buying and connecting a domain](/blog/buy-and-connect-a-domain/) explains it in plain words.
7. **Check it.** Run the free [website grader](/grader/) for phone speed, search basics and whether AI search engines can read it.
8. **Change one thing a week.** Look at what visitors actually do, then improve the step where they stop.

## Mistakes that cost non-coders the most

- **The endless first version.** Polishing for weeks before anyone sees it. Launch to ten people first.
- **Secrets pasted into chat.** API keys and passwords belong in a secrets store, not a message. Our [security checklist](/blog/no-code-security-checklist/) has 15 questions to ask any tool.
- **Data that disappears.** A form that "works" but saves nowhere. Ask where each submission goes.
- **Nothing measured.** If you cannot see visits and sign-ups, you cannot improve. See [three numbers a small product should track](/blog/metrics-for-a-small-product/).
