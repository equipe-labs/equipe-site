Most "start a business with AI" advice is a list of tools: one for the logo, one for the copy, one for the website, one for the emails. You end up as the glue between ten subscriptions, which is a job in itself. I started equipe because I think the more useful question is different: what if one person could have a whole startup team, and only make the decisions?

This guide is how I would start a small business with AI today if I could not code. It uses what we have seen in real projects on equipe, including the numbers, and it is honest about what AI does badly.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> To start a business with AI, keep the decisions yourself (who it is for, what you promise, the price) and hand the production work to AI: building the website or app, putting it live, writing drafts and measuring what visitors do. Check the idea in an afternoon, launch the smallest version in a day, and ask real people to pay within a month.</p>

## What "starting a business with AI" really means

There are three different things people mean, and they need different tools.

| What you want | AI's role | Good first step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use AI to run an ordinary business (a tutor, a salon, a coach) | Builds the site and booking, drafts posts, answers questions | A [website guide for your business type](/websites/) |
| Start a business whose product is software | Builds and runs the product itself | [How to build a website or app without coding](/blog/build-a-website-or-app-without-coding/) |
| Sell an AI-powered service | AI is part of what you sell, for example a chatbot for clients | [AI features small businesses can add](/blog/ai-features-for-small-business-websites/) |

In all three, the business is the same old thing: someone has a problem, you solve it, they pay. AI changes how much one person can do, not what a business is.

## What an AI startup team is

**An AI startup team is a group of AI agents in startup roles that build a product, put it live and keep improving it, while one person approves anything that goes live, costs money or reaches customers.**

On equipe the roles are a PM who turns your goal into a short plan, engineers and a designer who build, QA who checks before release, and a data analyst who reads how people use it. The difference from a single chat assistant is that the work is split, tracked and checked, and it continues after the first version. I describe each role in [what an AI startup team actually does](/blog/what-an-ai-startup-team-does/), and the claims to be sceptical of in [AI agents that run your startup: what it really means](/blog/what-ai-agents-running-a-startup-really-means/).

## What you decide, and what AI does

| You decide | AI does |
| --- | --- |
| Who the customer is and the one problem you solve | Researches competitors and drafts the first plan |
| What you promise and what you charge | Builds the site or app, forms, sign-in, admin |
| What goes live, what costs money, what reaches customers | Puts it live: hosting, database, domain, privacy policy |
| Accounts in your name: payments, domain, bank | Writes first drafts of copy and posts |
| When to stop, change direction or double down | Measures visits and sign-ups and proposes the next change |

That split is not just a safety rule. It is where the value is. Your judgment about customers is the thing AI cannot supply.

## A plan for your first 30 days

1. **Day 1: check the idea in an afternoon.** Run it through the free [idea validator](/tools/idea-validator/) for an honest look at competitors and the riskiest assumption, find a name with a live domain check in the [business name generator](/business-name-generator/), and see what the product would cost to build in the [app cost calculator](/tools/app-cost-calculator/). The full routine is in [before you build: check your idea, name and costs in an afternoon](/blog/business-idea-to-plan-in-an-afternoon/).
2. **Days 2 to 7: talk to ten people.** Ask about the problem, not your solution. Our [12 interview questions](/blog/validate-startup-idea-interview-questions/) help.
3. **Week 2: launch the smallest version.** One page and one action: book, join, buy or ask. On equipe the median first version went live in about 40 minutes.
4. **Week 3: launch to ten people, personally.** Send the link yourself. Watch what they do.
5. **Week 4: ask for money.** Even a small price teaches you more than a hundred compliments. See [how to price your first product](/blog/how-to-price-your-first-product/).

The week-by-week version with the traps to avoid is in [from idea to paying customers: a 30-day plan](/blog/idea-to-paying-customers-30-day-plan/).

## What it costs, with real numbers

Software is now the cheap part. From equipe's records of real customer projects (as of 27 November 2026):

- **First live version:** median 477 credits, about $4.77 (11 projects; the middle half used 319 to 1,329 credits). The first version is on us.
- **First week of work:** median 3,290 credits, about $32.90 (13 projects).
- **Keeping it live:** $19 a month on the Team plan, or $69 a month with 5,000 credits of monthly work, where most teams start.

Compare that with the 2026 agency guides our calculator cites: about $3,650 to $12,750 for a small business website and $15,000 to $50,000 for a booking app. I compare the routes properly in [AI team vs freelancer vs agency](/blog/ai-team-vs-freelancer-vs-agency/), and the line items are in [what it costs to build and run a small app with AI agents](/blog/cost-to-build-and-run-a-small-app-with-ai-agents/).

Your other costs do not go away: a domain (usually $10 to $20 a year), card processing fees, any paid data service, and your own time.

## A real example

Golden Boot Academy, a youth soccer training academy in the San Francisco Bay Area, came to equipe with a site it had already made with an AI tool. Its AI team rebuilt it as a site it could keep changing, then added player sign-up and accounts, booking requests saved to an admin page, real FAQ answers and its own domain. Within its first week live, real visitors were signing up and sending real booking requests. It was not all smooth, and [the case study](/blog/golden-boot-academy-case-study/) says what went wrong too.
