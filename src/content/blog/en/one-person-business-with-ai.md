The idea of a one-person company has been around for years. What changed in 2026 is that the work you used to hire for first, a developer to build the product and someone to keep it running, can now be done by AI agents. I build equipe around exactly that idea, and I also run it largely with AI teams: equipe's own site and apps were built by equipe teams, with one person approving.

This guide is what I have learned about what to hand off and what to keep.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> One person can run a small business with AI by handing off production work (building and running the website or app, drafts, reports) and keeping the decisions (customers, prices, money, what goes public). Budget about an hour a day, and approve rather than operate.</p>

## What to hand off

| Work | Hand to AI? | What you still do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building the website or app | Yes | Describe it; approve what goes live |
| Hosting, database, domain, sign-in | Yes, to a team that runs it | Approve the domain's yearly price |
| Fixes and small changes | Yes | Report what is wrong in plain words |
| Measuring visits and sign-ups | Yes | Read a weekly report; pick the next goal |
| First drafts of posts, emails, FAQ answers | Yes | Edit for your voice; approve before sending |
| Sorting enquiries (hot, warm, cold) | Yes | Answer the hot ones yourself |
| Privacy policy and terms from your real features | Yes, as a draft | Have a lawyer check if the stakes are high |

## What to keep

- **Talking to customers.** Ten conversations teach you more than any dashboard. Our [interview questions](/blog/validate-startup-idea-interview-questions/) help.
- **Prices.** See [how to price your first product](/blog/how-to-price-your-first-product/).
- **Money and contracts.** Payment processor accounts, refunds, anything you sign.
- **What goes public.** Releases, emails to customers, posts. On equipe these wait for your approval by design; [approval cards](/blog/approval-cards-explained/) explain how.
- **Saying no.** AI teams will happily build more. Most small businesses need less.

## An hour a day

A rhythm that works for a solo founder with an AI team:

1. **20 minutes with customers.** Reply, call, ask one question.
2. **20 minutes approving.** Review what the team proposes, look at the preview, approve or push back.
3. **20 minutes on one improvement.** Pick the step where visitors stop, and ask the team to fix just that.

Once a week, read the growth report and choose next week's single goal. The loop is described in [build, measure, learn with an AI team](/blog/build-measure-learn-with-an-ai-team/).

## What it costs to run

From equipe's records of real customer projects (as of 27 November 2026): the median first live version used 477 credits (about $4.77), the median first week 3,290 credits (about $32.90), and the median single change after launch 130 credits (about $1.30). Keeping a product live is $19 a month; most teams that keep building choose 5,000 credits a month for $69.

Compare that with hiring: a part-time freelancer for ongoing changes usually bills by the hour, and each change is another quote. I compare the options in [AI team vs freelancer vs agency](/blog/ai-team-vs-freelancer-vs-agency/).

## The honest limits

- **AI agents make confident mistakes.** A real example: on one customer project, the PM estimated the first sprint at about $2 and it used about $8. Read the cost before approving and set a monthly cap.
- **They do extra work if you let them.** On the same project, the team restyled a site nobody asked it to restyle. Be explicit about what not to change.
- **They cannot invent what does not exist.** If the data or the market is not there, no amount of building fixes it.
- **Someone has to be accountable.** That is you, and it should be.
