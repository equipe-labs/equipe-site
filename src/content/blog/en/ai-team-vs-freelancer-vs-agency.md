Before AI, a non-coder with a product idea had two choices: hire a freelancer or hire an agency. Now there is a third, an AI startup team. I build one, so read this with that in mind. I have tried to be fair: there are real cases where a person is the better choice, and I say which.

The agency and freelancer figures come from published 2026 price guides (dated, listed under Sources). The AI-team figures come from equipe's records of real customer projects.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> An AI startup team is the fastest and cheapest way to get a first version live and to keep changing it every week. A freelancer is best for bespoke work on a fixed scope. An agency is best for a large, brand-critical project with many stakeholders. Many small businesses combine a human for the brand with an AI team for the building.</p>

## Side by side

| | AI startup team (equipe) | Freelancer | Agency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First version of a small business website | Median 477 credits (about $4.77); the first version is on us | About $3,650 to $12,750 for an agency or freelancer (2026 guides) | Same range, toward the top |
| Time to a live first version | Median about 40 minutes (11 projects) | 2 to 8 weeks (2026 guides) | 2 to 8 weeks, often longer |
| A change after launch | Median 130 credits (about $1.30) per change (150 changes) | Hourly or a new quote | A change request and a quote |
| Hosting, domain, database | Included and run by the team | Often left to you | Often a separate retainer |
| Measuring and proposing improvements | Weekly report with suggestions you approve | Only if you pay for it | Usually a separate service |
| Bespoke brand and judgment | Good, follows your brand; not a brand agency | Strong, depends on the person | Strongest |
| Meetings with your stakeholders | No | Yes | Yes |
| Accountability | You approve everything that goes live | The person, under contract | The company, under contract |

## When an AI team is the right choice

- You want something live this week, not this quarter.
- You expect to change it often: new offers, new pages, fixes after customers try it.
- You do not want to manage hosting, a domain or a database.
- Your budget is small and you would rather spend it on customers.

Golden Boot Academy, a youth soccer academy, is a typical example: its AI team rebuilt an existing site in about 14 minutes and had it live with accounts, bookings and its own domain within three days. [The case study](/blog/golden-boot-academy-case-study/) includes what went wrong.

## When a person is the right choice

- **A distinctive brand.** If the look is the product, hire a designer.
- **Old systems.** A complex link to an on-premises system or an unusual vendor needs someone who can call people.
- **Regulated work.** Medical, legal or financial products may need a named professional to sign off.
- **Stakeholders.** If five people must agree, you need someone who runs meetings.

## Questions to ask any of the three

1. What exactly is included in the price, and what is extra?
2. Who hosts it, and what does that cost each month?
3. What does a small change cost after launch, and how fast is it?
4. Who owns the code, the data and the domain? Can I export them?
5. How will I know whether it is working (visits, sign-ups, bookings)?
6. What happens if you disappear, or I stop paying?
