Most first-time founders do it in the wrong order: they build for weeks, then find out who wants it. With AI, building is now so fast that it is tempting to skip the checking altogether. Please do not. An afternoon of checking saves weeks of building the wrong thing.

This is the routine I suggest to friends. Every tool in it is free and needs no sign-up; most of them are on equipe.im because we built them for exactly this moment.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> In one afternoon you can write your idea in one sentence, run an honest check on it, pick a name with an available domain, estimate what building it would cost, draft a privacy policy and list ten people to ask. Then spend a week talking to those people before you build.</p>

## Hour 1: the idea in one sentence, then an honest check

Write it like this: "**[Who]** has **[problem]**, and today they **[current workaround]**. I will **[what you do]**." For example: "Parents of young soccer players want better coaching feedback, and today they film matches on their phones and guess. I will review a match video and send a plan."

Then paste it into the free [startup idea validator](/tools/idea-validator/). In about 30 seconds it gives you who it is for, the problem, real competitors from a live web search (kept only when their site actually appeared in the results), the riskiest assumption, a 7-day test plan and a first-version scope.

Read the riskiest assumption twice. That is what your week of conversations should test.

## Hour 2: a name and a domain

Describe the business in the [business name generator](/business-name-generator/). You get about ten ideas, each with a live .com and .co check and the yearly price. Choose one you can say out loud, spell after hearing it, and get a domain for. Do not buy anything yet.

Two quick checks: search the name with your city or category to see who else uses it, and look on the social networks your customers use.

## Hour 3: what building it would cost

Pick the kind of product in the [app cost calculator](/tools/app-cost-calculator/): landing page, website, online store, booking app, marketplace, SaaS, mobile app, internal tool, community or directory. Tick the features you really need. It shows three routes side by side:

- an agency or freelancer, from dated 2026 price guides;
- a no-code tool stack, from list prices;
- equipe, from real customer project costs.

For a sense of scale: the median first live version on equipe used 477 credits (about $4.77) across 11 real projects, as of 27 November 2026. If the number for your idea is large, that is a signal to cut the first version down.

## Hour 4: the pieces customers will see first

- **A privacy policy.** If you will collect even an email address, you need one. The [privacy policy generator](/tools/privacy-policy-generator/) writes a plain-language policy from your answers, with GDPR, CCPA and other sections when they apply. It is not legal advice.
- **A booking page,** if you sell time. The [booking page maker](/tools/booking-page/) shows a working preview as you type and gives you a share link.
- **Ten names.** Write down ten real people who have the problem. Not friends who will be nice: people who would pay.

## The week after

1. Talk to the ten people about the problem, not your idea. Use our [12 interview questions](/blog/validate-startup-idea-interview-questions/).
2. If at least a few say they have the problem and have tried to solve it, build the smallest version. On equipe you can paste your idea and the PM starts from it; your first version is on us.
3. Ask for money early. A real offer is the only test that counts.
