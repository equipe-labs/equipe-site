## What each one says it is

- **Lovable** describes itself in its docs as "a full-stack AI development platform for building, iterating on, and deploying web applications."
- **Bolt** says "Create stunning apps & websites by chatting with AI."
- **Base44** says "Build your own apps, websites, products and AI agents on Base44 using your own words," and its docs call it "a no-code AI platform for building full-stack apps, websites, and AI agents."
- **equipe** is an AI startup team for people who cannot code: a PM, engineers, a designer, QA and a data analyst, all AI agents, who build, launch and keep improving your product. You approve anything that goes live, costs money or reaches your customers.

The first three are builders you drive prompt by prompt. equipe is a team you direct like a cofounder. That difference shapes everything below.

## Where the builders are strong

Be clear-eyed about this. For the thing they are built for, the builders are good.

- **Speed to a first version.** You describe an app and see it quickly. Lovable, Bolt and Base44 all include hosting and a backend, so a first version can exist within minutes.
- **A built-in backend.** Lovable's docs describe a database, sign-in, file storage and functions in Lovable Cloud. Bolt documents a built-in database with email and Google sign-in. Base44 lists an integrated backend and database on every plan.
- **Visible code and Git.** Lovable and Base44 document two-way GitHub sync, which is valuable if you ever hire a developer.
- **Security paperwork.** Bolt and Base44 state SOC 2 Type 2 certification on their security pages, which matters for companies with procurement checklists. equipe does not claim that today.
- **Low entry prices.** Base44's Starter is $16 a month billed annually; Lovable and Bolt start at $25.

## Where a team is different

Credit where it is due: the questions below are about the gap *after* the first version, which is where equipe aims.

- **Who decides what to build next?** In a builder you do, prompt by prompt. In equipe, the data analyst reads how people use the product, and the PM proposes the next improvement in plain words.
- **Who runs it?** With a builder you pick plans, domains and publishing settings. With equipe, the team sets up hosting, a database and the domain, and keeps it running; you never sign in to a code host or cloud console.
- **Who checks the work?** equipe has a QA role that tests the way customers would, separate from the engineers who wrote the code.
- **What waits for you?** In equipe, going live, spending money and messaging your customers always wait for your approval. See [what approvals mean](/blog/is-equipe-safe-data-money-approvals/).

Base44 does describe "Superagents", always-on personal agents that run tasks for you using scheduled jobs and triggers. As far as we could read, those are task agents connected to your tools; we found no statement that they keep improving the app you built, so we do not treat them as the same thing. We may be wrong, and Base44 may change this.

## Where equipe is weaker (honestly)

- **Newer and smaller.** The category and the product are young. The site says exactly what works today and what does not.
- **Not shipped yet:** one-step import from Claude with a database and a domain, and weekly usage reports with proposals. Today you share code or describe it and the team moves it for you.
- **Less self-serve control.** Builders let you edit the code and push to GitHub on your own. equipe asks you to be the decision-maker, not the editor.
- **No SOC 2 or ISO 27001 claim.** If your company requires one, equipe is not the right pick yet.
- **Pricing is two-part.** A plan plus credits for AI work. Every request shows its expected cost first and you set a monthly cap, but it is more to understand than a flat fee. Our [cost breakdown](/blog/cost-to-build-and-run-a-small-app-with-ai-agents/) walks through it.
