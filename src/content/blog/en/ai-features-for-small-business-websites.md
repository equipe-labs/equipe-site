AI on a small business website used to mean a clumsy chat bubble that answered nothing. In 2026 the useful features are smaller and more specific: answer the question a visitor actually asked, summarize what a hundred reviews say, draft the reply you were going to write anyway.

Here are eleven that a small business can add today, grouped by who they help, with when each is worth it and what to watch out for. None of them needs you to write code.

<p class="answer"><strong>The short answer.</strong> The most useful AI features for a small business site are a support chatbot, instant FAQ answers, translation, smart form filling and recommendations for visitors; review summaries, product descriptions, reply drafts and post images for you; and inquiry sorting and comment checks behind the scenes. Start with the one that removes the most repeated work, and set a monthly spending limit first.</p>

## For your visitors, on your site

### 1. A support chatbot

Answers questions about what you offer, prices, hours, delivery and policies, at any hour and in the visitor's language.

- **Worth it when** your inbox fills with the same questions.
- **Watch out for** answers it makes up. Give it your facts in writing and tell it to say when it isn't sure. Our guide on [adding an AI chatbot without coding](/blog/add-ai-chatbot-to-website-without-coding/) goes step by step.

### 2. Instant FAQ answers

A question box above your FAQ: the visitor types in their own words and gets the matching answer in two or three sentences.

- **Worth it when** you have a long FAQ that people don't read.
- **Watch out for** an out-of-date FAQ. The answers are only as good as the page.

### 3. Site translation

Turns a page into the visitor's language while keeping names, prices and links as they are.

- **Worth it when** you get visitors from other countries, or serve a city with several languages.
- **Watch out for** legal pages and anything with strict wording. Have those checked by a person.

### 4. Smart form fill

Visitors paste an email or a note ("table for 2 on Friday, ana@mail.com") and the form fills itself.

- **Worth it when** your forms are long, such as bookings, quotes or applications.
- **Watch out for** guesses. It should leave a field empty rather than invent an email address or a phone number.

### 5. Recommendations

The visitor says what they need ("a gift under $40") and gets two or three of your own products or services with a line on why each fits.

- **Worth it when** you have more than a handful of products and people ask "which one should I choose?"
- **Watch out for** suggestions outside your catalog. It should only pick from what you sell.

## For you and your staff, in your admin

### 6. Review and booking summaries

Turns a long list of reviews or bookings into the overall mood, what people praise, the most common complaints and one suggestion.

- **Worth it when** you have more reviews than time to read them.
- **Watch out for** treating the summary as the full story. Read the reviews behind a surprising point.

### 7. Product descriptions

Write a few notes ("handmade, 350 ml, dishwasher safe") and get a clear description with the key facts.

- **Worth it when** you add products often.
- **Watch out for** made-up claims. Check every number and promise before you publish.

### 8. Reply drafts

A first draft of your answer to a customer email, in their language, with a note wherever you need to check something.

- **Worth it when** you answer many similar messages.
- **Watch out for** sending without reading. A draft saves typing, not judgment.

### 9. Images for your posts

Describe a picture for a social post or your blog and get a clean, well-lit image.

- **Worth it when** you post regularly and don't have photos for everything.
- **Watch out for** passing it off as a real photo of your shop or product. Use real photos for those.

## Behind the scenes

### 10. Inquiry triage

Each new inquiry is marked hot, warm or cold, with the reason and the best next step.

- **Worth it when** inquiries arrive faster than you can answer them all the same day.
- **Watch out for** ignoring "cold" ones completely. Skim them now and then.

### 11. Comment and review checks

Checks what people post on your site before it appears: fine, needs a look, or blocked, with a few words on why.

- **Worth it when** visitors can post reviews, comments or listings.
- **Watch out for** over-blocking. Look at what was held back every week.

## How to choose, and keep the cost in check

1. **Start with one feature.** Pick the one that removes the most repeated work.
2. **Give it your own facts.** Prices, hours, policies, catalog. An AI that doesn't know your business will guess.
3. **Set a monthly limit before launch,** plus limits per visitor on anything public.
4. **Keep keys and instructions off the page.** An AI key in your site's code can be copied and spent by anyone.
5. **Tell visitors it's AI** and that answers can be wrong.
6. **Read what happens in the first two weeks,** then decide on the next feature.

## What's still early

Some things are less ready for a small site than the demos suggest. Voice assistants that take phone calls need careful testing. Search by meaning across a whole site needs an index that is kept up to date. Fully automatic replies sent without a person reading them are risky for anything involving money or promises. Start with the text features above.
