## What Base44 is, and what it is good at

Base44's homepage says "Build your own apps, websites, products and AI agents on Base44 using your own words." Its docs call it "a no-code AI platform for building full-stack apps, websites, and AI agents." In plain terms: a **builder**. You type what you want, it produces a working app, and you keep typing to change it.

From its official pages on 21 November 2026, these are real strengths:

- **The lowest entry price shown.** Starter is $16 a month, billed annually (the page shows no monthly figures). Of the builders we compared, that is the cheapest paid entry we read.
- **An integrated backend on every plan.** Database, storage, sign-in and analytics come built in, so you do not wire up separate services.
- **Security paperwork.** Its security page states SOC 2 Type II "confirmed by independent audit", ISO 27001 certification, and GDPR with a data processing agreement on request.
- **Code sync.** Two-way GitHub sync that exports the full source code to your own repository is listed on Builder, Pro and Elite, not on Starter or Free.
- **Custom domains** from Starter up, with a free domain for one year on paid plans.
- **Superagents.** Covered in its own section below.

If your goal is a quick, cheap first version that you will keep steering, these are good reasons to stay.

## Base44 plans at a glance

All figures below are as read on 21 November 2026, billed annually.

| Plan | Price | Message credits / month | Integration credits / month | GitHub sync |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | 25 | 100 (up to 5 apps) | No |
| Starter | $16/month | 100 | 2,000 | No |
| Builder | $40/month | 250 | 10,000 | Yes |
| Pro | $80/month | 500 | 20,000 | Yes |
| Elite | $160/month | 1,200 | 50,000 | Yes |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom | Custom | Not stated |

Two details matter. First, your app's size of work is measured in credits, so heavier use moves you up a tier. Second, GitHub sync starts at Builder, which is $40 a month on this page, so "cheapest" and "code in your own repo" are not the same plan.

## What Superagents are, and what they are not

Base44 has a page for "Superagents": "A personal AI agent that runs tasks for you 24/7," using scheduled jobs, event triggers and recurring tasks. We found no pricing on that page.

Our reading, which may be wrong: Superagents are **personal task agents**. They do jobs for you, such as things on a schedule or when something happens. We found **no statement that they improve the app you built**, for example by reading how customers use it and changing it. That is a different job from the one we describe for a team. Base44 may well add this or may already do it somewhere we did not read. If it matters to your decision, ask them directly.

## When to stay with Base44

Staying is the right call more often than a "best alternatives" article will admit. Stay if:

- **You enjoy steering.** If prompting and previewing is the fun part, an extra layer between you and the app is a cost, not a benefit.
- **The app is small and stable.** A personal tool or internal tracker that rarely changes does not need a team.
- **You need certifications on paper.** If a customer's checklist asks for SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001, Base44 states both on its security page. equipe claims neither.
- **You want code in your own repository at a low price.** Builder with two-way GitHub sync may be exactly right.
- **You are still exploring.** Cheap and fast beats careful when you are not sure what you are building yet.

## When to look elsewhere

These are signs that a builder, any builder, is starting to feel tight:

1. **You are the only project manager.** Every change starts with you deciding, writing a prompt and checking the result. If that has become your second job, you may want a PM who asks questions and plans.
2. **Nobody tests like a customer.** If you keep finding bugs after you publish, you may want a separate checker, not the same tool that wrote the change.
3. **You never look at usage.** Shipping is half the loop. If nobody reads how people use the product and proposes what to change, improvement depends on your free evenings.
4. **You cannot tell what is safe to publish.** You want a gate: nothing goes live, costs money or reaches customers without you saying yes.
5. **You want the setup handled.** Hosting, database and domain decisions feel like homework.

If two or more of these ring true, a team is worth a look. Our guide to [builders versus teams](/blog/claude-artifact-vs-app-builder-vs-ai-team/) explains the difference in more depth, and [what an AI startup team does](/blog/what-an-ai-startup-team-does/) walks through the roles.

## Where equipe fits

equipe is an AI startup team for people who cannot code: a PM, engineers, a designer, QA and a data analyst, all AI agents. The PM asks questions first, turns your idea into a plan and keeps the team on it. The team works from a shared board of tickets, and each member works on its own copy of the project, so nothing merges into your main product until you say so.

What that means in practice:

- Anything that goes live, costs money or reaches customers waits for your approval, shown as a short card with options, a recommendation and the expected cost. One tap answers it. See [approval cards explained](/blog/approval-cards-explained/).
- The team sets up hosting, the database and the domain, one project per customer on Cloudflare. You never sign in to a code host or cloud console. A domain purchase needs an approval only a signed-in member of your team can give.
- Analytics for the product you build are cookie-free and store no IP address.
- Pricing: The Team plan starts at $19 a month ($190 a year) to keep your product live, and you choose a monthly credit amount on top; most teams start with $50 of credits for $69 a month, with hosting and storage included; everything the team does is paid from credits, with AI work shown to the cent per task. You set a monthly cap. There is a free start with a $5 credit, no card needed. Details on [the pricing page](/pricing/).

## Where equipe is weaker

We would rather you know this before you sign up:

- **No SOC 2 or ISO 27001 claim.** Base44 states both. If you need them, stay or go with a builder that has them.
- **No published backup or uptime commitment, and no public status page.**
- **No GitHub two-way sync advertised.** Base44's Builder plan documents it. With equipe, a product that outgrows our hosting can be moved to your own accounts, and the engine behind it is open source (MIT), but that is not the same as live sync.
- **Not shipped yet:** one-step import from Claude with a database and a domain, and weekly usage reports with proposals. Until then you share the code or describe it and the team moves it for you, and the PM can review usage and propose when asked.
- **Newer, with a two-part price.** A plan plus credits. Every request shows its cost first, but a flat builder fee is simpler. Our [cost breakdown](/blog/cost-to-build-and-run-a-small-app-with-ai-agents/) walks through it.

## A simple way to decide

| Your situation | Likely fit |
| --- | --- |
| Small app, you like prompting, tight budget | Stay with Base44 |
| Need SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 stated | Base44 (as on its security page) |
| Need live code sync to your repo | Base44 Builder or higher |
| Want agents to plan, test and propose, with you approving | equipe |
| Prototype in a builder, then hand off | Both: build there, bring the result to a team |

Prototyping in Base44 and bringing the result to a team later is a normal path, not a betrayal.

Before you switch anything, write down what you want to be true six months from now: who decides, who tests, who reads usage, who pays for what. If a builder can do that, keep it.
